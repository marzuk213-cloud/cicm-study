📊 Bedside Measurement — Ventilator Waveform

Inspiratory hold on a vented patient. Pplat at zero flow → Cstat (elastic only). Ppeak during flow → Cdyn (includes resistance). The gap between them = airway resistance.

Vt = 500 mL · PEEP = 5 · Ppeak = 28 · Pplat = 20
Cstat = Vt / (Pplat − PEEP) = 500 / (20 − 5) = 33 mL/cmH₂O
Cdyn = Vt / (Ppeak − PEEP) = 500 / (28 − 5) = 22 mL/cmH₂O
Cstat − Cdyn gap widens with ↑ Raw or heterogeneous lung disease (asthma, ARDS).
Oesophageal balloon → partitions lung from chest wall: CL = Vt / (Pplat − Poes); Ccw = Vt / Poes. Used in advanced ARDS.

📐 Definitions & Components

Compliance = ΔV / ΔP · units mL/cmH₂O. Elastance = 1/C = ΔP/ΔV. High C = floppy lung; low C = stiff lung.

Specific compliance = CL / FRC ≈ 0.05 cmH₂O⁻¹. Size-corrected — allows comparison between patients of different lung volumes. Matters because absolute C scales with lung size.

⏱️ Static vs Dynamic Compliance

2014A 15 examiner pearl (34% pass): "Many candidates failed to clearly demonstrate the difference between static and dynamic compliance. Many had little knowledge of how compliance is measured."

Static (Cstat)

  • Zero-flow conditions (inspiratory hold)
  • Reflects elastic properties only
  • Cstat = Vt / (Pplat − PEEPtot)
  • Used to guide PEEP / lung-protective ventilation

Dynamic (Cdyn)

  • Measured during tidal breathing
  • Includes airway resistance component
  • Cdyn = Vt / (Ppeak − PEEPtot)
  • Frequency-dependent: ↑RR → long τ alveoli can't fill → effective C falls
Cstat ≥ Cdyn always. Gap widens with ↑Raw, ↑RR, heterogeneous disease (asthma, ARDS). Time constant τ = R × C.

🧪 Surfactant — Laplace Mechanism (Click "Next" to reveal)

Composition: 85% phospholipid (DPPC main surface-active) · 10% protein (SP-A, B, C, D) · 5% neutral lipid. Made by Type II pneumocytes.

1Laplace's law applied to alveoli
P = 2T/r. For a liquid sphere, smaller radius → higher collapse pressure. Without surfactant: small alveoli would empty into larger ones (Pascal's network effect).
2Surfactant orientation at the air-liquid interface
Hydrophilic head sits in aqueous phase, hydrophobic tail projects into airspace. Disrupts intermolecular water H-bonds → reduces surface tension.
3Concentration effect (the key examiner point)
As alveolus shrinks, surfactant molecules are pushed closer together → tension-lowering effect is more potent at smaller radii. Result: T falls faster than r → P stays stable → alveolus doesn't collapse.
4Multi-functional consequences
↓ Surface tension → ↑ Lung compliance · maintains FRC · provides hysteresis · stabilises alveoli (prevents derecruitment) · opposes Starling forces (keeps fluid out of alveoli).
5Causes of surfactant deficiency → ↓CL
IRDS (premature, type II immaturity) · ARDS (plasma protein inactivation) · smoke inhalation · near-drowning · prolonged bypass (washout).

⚠️ Examiner Pitfalls — Free Marks Lost By Most Candidates

1. Reversing the recoil-compliance arrows. Fibrosis = stiff = ↑recoil = ↓CL. Emphysema = floppy = ↓recoil = ↑CL. (Your prior attempts had this reversed — anchor it.)
2. Listing without explaining mechanism. Every factor needs a "because…" clause. "Obesity ↓Ccw because mass loading + ↑IAP."
3. Not bifurcating lung vs chest wall. Use two separate tables/sections. Examiner explicitly rewards this division.
4. Missing measurement methodology. 2011B made measurement worth 70% of marks. Specify how Pplat, Ppeak, PEEP, Vt, Poes are measured.
5. Hysteresis with one limb only. Must draw BOTH inflation and deflation. Deflation sits above inflation. Causes: surfactant recruitment, viscoelasticity, alveolar recruitment.
6. Forgetting the freebies. Units (mL/cmH₂O), elastance = 1/C, specific compliance (C/FRC), series equation 1/Crs = 1/CL + 1/Ccw — all explicit easy marks.

🃏 Flashcards — Tap to flip (12 cards, targets prior weak areas)

Define respiratory compliance with units.
Compliance = ΔV/ΔP, mL/cmH₂O. It is the reciprocal of elastance.
State the series equation for total respiratory compliance.
1/Crs = 1/CL + 1/Ccw. With Crs ≈ 100, CL ≈ 200, Ccw ≈ 200, the equation balances.
Define specific compliance and why it matters.
Specific C = CL / FRC (~0.05 cmH₂O⁻¹). Size-corrected — allows comparison between patients of different lung volumes since absolute C scales with lung size.
FIBROSIS — arrow direction for recoil and compliance?
Fibrosis = STIFF = ↑ elastic recoil = ↓ CL. (Anchor: stiff lung needs more pressure for same volume.)
EMPHYSEMA — arrow direction for recoil and compliance?
Emphysema = FLOPPY = ↓ elastic recoil = ↑ CL. (Loss of elastic tissue.)
Why does Cstat ≥ Cdyn always?
Cstat is measured at zero flow (elastic only). Cdyn is measured during flow and additionally includes airway resistance, so the denominator (Ppeak − PEEP) is larger.
How does surfactant work via Laplace's law? (Concentration effect)
As alveolus shrinks, surfactant molecules are pushed closer together → tension-lowering effect is more potent at smaller radii. T falls faster than r → P stays stable → small alveoli don't collapse into large.
Why does pulmonary blood volume ↑ reduce CL?
Vascular engorgement increases lung tissue stiffness (interstitial pressure rises, vessels physically take space). Seen in LVF, supine, Trendelenburg, fluid overload.
How is Cstat measured on a ventilator (with equation)?
Inspiratory hold (≥0.5s) until flow stops → read plateau pressure (Pplat). Cstat = Vt / (Pplat − PEEPtot).
How does oesophageal balloon allow partitioning into CL vs Ccw?
Poes ≈ Ppl. Therefore CL = Vt / (Pplat − Poes); Ccw = Vt / Poes. Used in advanced ARDS strategies.
Why does frequency-dependent compliance fall with ↑RR in heterogeneous lung disease?
Units with long time constants (τ = R×C, e.g., fibrotic or partially obstructed alveoli) cannot fill at high RR. Volume distributes only to fast units → apparent Cdyn falls.
Two causes of ↑ chest wall compliance.
Flail chest / sternotomy (loss of structural integrity); connective tissue diseases like Marfan/EDS (↑ extensibility); also neonates relative to adults (cartilaginous ribs).
