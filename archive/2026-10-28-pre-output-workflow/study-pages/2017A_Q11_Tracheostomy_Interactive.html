② Layers — Skin → Trachea (Needle Path)

Click "Next layer" to reveal each step. The avascular midline raphe is the whole game.

1
Skin
Incision typically 1-2 cm vertical at level of rings 1-2 (perc) or 2-3/3-4 (surgical).
2
Subcutaneous tissue + Platysma
Variable fat. Platysma is a thin sheet in superficial fascia — innervated by facial nerve (cervical branch).
3
Investing layer of deep cervical fascia
Encloses SCM and trapezius laterally; in midline it splits to enclose strap muscles.
4
Strap muscles + LINEA ALBA CERVICALIS
Sternohyoid (superficial) and sternothyroid (deep). Separated in midline by the avascular linea alba cervicalis — examiner buzzword #1. Stay midline = bloodless dissection.
5
Pretracheal fascia
Encloses thyroid gland and contains the pretracheal venous plexus (inferior thyroid veins).
6
Thyroid isthmus (rings 2-4)
Retract superiorly or divide between clamps. Berry's ligament anchors thyroid to cricoid + upper rings — RLN risk here.
7
Fibroelastic annular ligament
Between tracheal rings — where the needle/scalpel enters the airway.
8
Trachea
Perc: rings 1-2 (or between 1st and 2nd). Surgical: rings 2-3 or 3-4.

③ Trachea — Core Structure

  • Definition: Fibrocartilaginous tube
  • Extent: C6 → T4/5 (sternal angle / carina)
  • Length: ~10 cm total; ~5 cm cervical, ~5 cm thoracic
  • Diameter: ~1.8–2.5 cm
  • Shape: D-shaped (flat posteriorly)
  • Rings: 16–20 incomplete C-shaped hyaline cartilage; connected by annular ligaments; trachealis muscle bridges posterior gap
  • Course: Becomes more posterior as it descends

⑤ Nerves & Blood Supply

Nerves

  • Recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN): tracheo-oesophageal groove bilaterally
    • Right loops under right subclavian a.
    • Left loops under aortic arch
  • Superior laryngeal nerve: external branch supplies cricothyroid (motor); internal branch sensory above cords
  • Vagus nerve: within carotid sheath (more lateral)
Mark phrase: "RLN lies in the tracheo-oesophageal groove — injured by lateral dissection or deep lateral needle trajectory."

Blood supply

  • Arterial: Inferior thyroid artery (upper ⅔); bronchial arteries (lower ⅓)
  • Venous: Inferior thyroid venous plexus → brachiocephalic veins
  • Lymph: Pretracheal + paratracheal nodes

⑥ Catastrophic Hazards

① Inferior thyroid veins / pretracheal venous plexus — most common bleeding source
② Thyroidea ima artery (3–10%) — unpaired midline vessel from brachiocephalic / aortic arch. Catches you out if you don't stay strictly midline.
③ Jugular venous arch — runs transversely across suprasternal space
④ Brachiocephalic (innominate) artery — tracheo-innominate fistula with low insertion → exsanguination 1–6 weeks post-op
⑤ Oesophageal perforation — posterior wall (membranous) breach

🃏 Active Recall — Flashcards

Tap each card to reveal. These target your last-attempt gaps.

Q: What is the avascular midline raphe between the strap muscles called?
A: Linea alba cervicalis. Examiner buzzword — staying within it = bloodless midline dissection.
Q: Name the two strap muscles divided by the linea alba cervicalis.
A: Sternohyoid (superficial) and sternothyroid (deep).
Q: What anchors the thyroid gland to the cricoid + upper tracheal rings?
A: Berry's ligament — RLN runs adjacent → key injury site.
Q: Tracheal blood supply — upper ⅔ vs lower ⅓?
A: Upper ⅔ → inferior thyroid artery. Lower ⅓ → bronchial arteries.
Q: Where does the right vs left RLN loop?
A: Right loops under right subclavian artery; left loops under aortic arch.
Q: Prevalence of thyroidea ima artery?
A: 3–10%. Unpaired midline vessel from brachiocephalic or aortic arch.
Q: Lymphatic drainage of the trachea?
A: Pretracheal + paratracheal nodes.
Q: At what vertebral level does the trachea begin and end?
A: C6 to T4/5 (sternal angle / carina).
Q: Catastrophic complication of low tracheostomy insertion?
A: Tracheo-innominate fistula — brachiocephalic a. erosion → exsanguination, classically 1-6 weeks post-op.
Q: Superior laryngeal nerve — motor vs sensory branches?
A: External branch → motor to cricothyroid. Internal branch → sensory above vocal cords (piercing thyrohyoid membrane).
