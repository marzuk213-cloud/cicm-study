🔑 Anchor: “Two classes block the transporter, one blocks the enzyme. TCA toxicity comes from what else they bind; SSRI toxicity comes from serotonin itself; MAOI toxicity comes from what the enzyme can no longer destroy.”

❤️ Why TCAs Kill: Na⁺ channel block → broad QRS Teaching model Published thresholds

TCAs bind the open/inactivated fast Na⁺ channel (quinidine-like, use-dependent). Phase 0 slows, conduction slows, and the QRS widens. K⁺ (IKr) block adds QT prolongation. Slide the dose; then add acidosis, tachycardia or bicarbonate.

Boehnert & Lovejoy thresholds19

  • Limb-lead QRS < 100 ms: no seizures or ventricular arrhythmias in their cohort.
  • ≥ 100 ms: seizures in 34%.
  • ≥ 160 ms: ventricular arrhythmias (14% of the ≥ 100 ms group).
  • Serum drug levels did not predict either.

Why bicarbonate works21

  • Na⁺ load raises the gradient across partially blocked channels.
  • Alkalaemia favours the un-ionised drug, which unbinds from the channel more easily (and protein binding rises).
  • Hypertonic saline or hyperventilation give part of the effect each.
  • Acidosis from seizures or hypoventilation does the reverse, so treat seizures fast (benzodiazepines).
Avoid in TCA overdose: physostigmine (bradyarrhythmia and asystole reported), flumazenil in mixed ingestions (seizures), class Ia and Ic antiarrhythmics (more Na⁺ block). Hypotension: fluids, bicarbonate, then a direct-acting vasopressor such as noradrenaline.20

🧬 MAO-A vs MAO-B, Selectivity and Reversibility

Both are FAD-containing flavoenzymes on the outer mitochondrial membrane. They oxidatively deaminate amines to aldehyde + NH₃ + H₂O₂.89

Preferred substrates5-HT, NA, adrenalineβ-phenylethylamine, benzylamine
Shared substratesDopamine and tyramine (in human striatum, most DA breakdown is by glial MAO-B)
Main sitesGut mucosa, liver, placenta, catecholaminergic neuronesPlatelets, astrocytes, serotonergic neurones, liver

Selective inhibitorsMoclobemide (reversible); methylene blue (potent, reversible)Selegiline, rasagiline (irreversible); safinamide (reversible)
Tyramine (“cheese”) riskYes: losing gut and liver MAO-A removes tyramine first-passLow at selective doses, because gut MAO-A is spared
Reversibility, mechanistically. Irreversible inhibitors (hydrazines like phenelzine; tranylcypromine; selegiline; rasagiline) form a covalent adduct with the FAD cofactor. The effect outlasts the drug, and recovery needs new enzyme synthesis, hence the ~2-week washout. In one study, oral tyramine sensitivity took ~30 days to normalise after tranylcypromine.10 A reversible competitive inhibitor (moclobemide) can be displaced by a large tyramine load, so the pressor response is far smaller.11

💊 Interaction Checker: the ICU version

Pick what the patient takes and what you are about to give. Each verdict names the mechanism, because that is what the examiners mark.671415

TCA + pressors

NET is blocked, so direct catecholamines (adrenaline, NA) linger: exaggerated response. Indirect agents (ephedrine, tyramine) can't get into the terminal: blunted.

MAOI + pressors

Vesicles are overfilled, so indirect agents release a huge NA bolus: crisis. Direct agents are mostly cleared by uptake and COMT, so they're modestly potentiated: start low and titrate.

MAOI + opioids

Pethidine, tramadol, methadone and dextromethorphan are weak SRIs: serotonin toxicity. Morphine, codeine, oxycodone and buprenorphine are not SRIs.14

🌡️ Serotonin Toxicity: a concentration-dependent spectrum

Serotonin toxicity is dose-related. Therapeutic SRIs cause side effects; an SRI overdose alone causes mild–moderate toxicity (about 14% of SSRI overdoses18). Life-threatening toxicity needs drugs acting by different mechanisms, classically an MAOI plus an SRI.14

Hunter criteria: tick the findings17

Applies only after a serotonergic agent. Sensitivity 84%, specificity 97% vs toxicologist diagnosis.

Management in one line each Published approach

  • Stop serotonergic drugs; supportive care. Most cases settle within 24 h.16
  • Benzodiazepines for agitation and muscle activity. Avoid physical restraint (it drives isometric contraction and heat).
  • Cyproheptadine (5-HT₂A antagonist, oral/NG only) for moderate cases; chlorpromazine is a parenteral alternative.
  • Severe (temperature > 38.5 °C, rising tone): intubate, paralyse with a non-depolarising agent, cool. The fever is muscle-generated, so antipyretics do not help.

⚠️ Exam Discriminators & Traps

1. “Cheese causes a hypotensive crisis.” It is hypertensive. But the commonest MAOI side effect at therapeutic dose is orthostatic hypotension. Write both, and keep them separate.
2. One list for side effects and overdose. Split them: TCA anticholinergic effects at therapeutic dose versus Na⁺-channel cardiotoxicity and seizures in overdose.
3. “Moclobemide is an MAO-B inhibitor.” No: reversible MAO-A (RIMA). Selegiline and rasagiline are the MAO-B drugs (Parkinson’s).
4. “SSRIs have no cardiac effects.” Class-level true; citalopram/escitalopram cause dose-dependent QT prolongation (FDA: citalopram max 40 mg/day, 20 mg/day over 60).23
5. “SSRI overdose causes serotonin syndrome, so SSRIs are dangerous.” Alone, mostly mild–moderate. Severe toxicity needs a combination (MAOI + SRI).14
6. “MAOIs potentiate all pressors.” The danger is indirect agents. And TCAs do the mirror image: they potentiate direct agents and blunt indirect ones.
7. Forgetting the hidden MAOIs. Linezolid (weak, non-selective) and methylene blue (potent MAO-A) cause serotonin toxicity with SRIs.15
8. Oversimplifying the monoamine hypothesis. The transporter or enzyme is blocked within hours; the mood effect takes weeks. Name the adaptive change (autoreceptor desensitisation).
9. “TCAs block dopamine reuptake.” Not meaningfully. TCAs = SERT + NET. Only MAOIs raise DA among these three.
10. Leaving out the beneficial side effects. The examiners gave extra marks for TCA analgesia, sedation, and SSRI “energising”.2

🃏 Flashcards: say it, then tap

One sentence: how do the three classes raise synaptic monoamines differently?
TCAs block SERT + NET; SSRIs block SERT only; MAOIs stop mitochondrial MAO breaking down amines inside the neurone, so stores and release increase.
Name the five TCA off-target actions and one consequence each.
M antagonism → dry mouth, retention, delirium. H₁ → sedation, weight. α₁ → orthostatic hypotension. Fast Na⁺ block → broad QRS, VT. K⁺ (hERG) block → long QT.
Why do SSRIs cause bleeding?
Platelets can't synthesise 5-HT; they take it up via SERT. SSRIs deplete platelet 5-HT, so 5-HT-amplified aggregation fails. Upper GI is the commonest site, worse with NSAIDs, antiplatelets or anticoagulants.
MAO-A vs MAO-B: substrates and sites?
A: 5-HT, NA (gut, liver, placenta, catecholaminergic neurones). B: PEA, benzylamine (platelets, astrocytes). Both: dopamine and tyramine.
Why is the tyramine reaction much less likely with moclobemide?
Reversible, competitive MAO-A inhibition: tyramine displaces it. Pressor sensitivity rises ~4× fasting vs 20–56× with irreversible MAOIs, and less again when taken after food.
Commonest therapeutic-dose MAOI side effect, and the proposed mechanism?
Orthostatic hypotension. Proposed: false transmitters (e.g. octopamine) displace NA in sympathetic vesicles, plus increased central α₂ tone.
Which opioids are dangerous with MAOIs and why?
Pethidine, tramadol, methadone, dextromethorphan: weak serotonin reuptake inhibitors → serotonin toxicity. Morphine, codeine, oxycodone, buprenorphine are not SRIs.
TCA vs MAOI: effect on ephedrine and on adrenaline?
TCA: ephedrine blunted (can't enter via NET), adrenaline exaggerated (uptake-1 blocked). MAOI: ephedrine dangerous (releases overfilled stores), adrenaline modestly potentiated.
Which SSRI has the cardiac problem, and what is it?
Citalopram (and escitalopram): dose-dependent QT prolongation. QTc > 440 ms in 68% of citalopram overdoses (Isbister 2004).
Why is the antidepressant effect delayed for weeks?
Acute rise in 5-HT activates somatodendritic 5-HT₁A (and α₂) autoreceptors that reduce firing and release. These desensitise over weeks; postsynaptic receptor changes and neuroplasticity follow.
Two hidden MAOIs in ICU?
Linezolid (weak, reversible, non-selective) and methylene blue (potent reversible MAO-A). Both can cause serotonin toxicity with SSRIs or SNRIs.
Fluoxetine and paroxetine share which kinetic interaction?
Potent CYP2D6 inhibition: ↑ TCAs, metoprolol, some antipsychotics; ↓ activation of codeine, tramadol, tamoxifen.
Three beneficial side effects the examiners rewarded?
TCA analgesia (neuropathic pain), sedation with TCAs or SSRIs, the energising effect of SSRIs/SNRIs.
Secondary vs tertiary amine TCAs?
Tertiary (amitriptyline, imipramine, clomipramine): more SERT, more anticholinergic, sedating. Demethylated to secondary amines (nortriptyline, desipramine): NET-selective, fewer side effects.
