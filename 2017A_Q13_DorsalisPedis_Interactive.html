⑤ The Four Mechanisms — Where 70% Lose Marks

Don't just list differences. Explain each with the underlying physics. Click cards for detail.

① Wave Reflection
Forward pressure wave reflects off high-resistance peripheral arterioles and bifurcations. Reflected wave summates with forward wave at distal sites → augmented systolic peak. The further from the heart, the more time for reflection to summate during systole.
② Impedance Mismatch
Progressive tapering and increased stiffness of distal muscular arteries creates an impedance mismatch at every transition (elastic → muscular). Each mismatch generates a reflected wave. By DPA, multiple reflections have summated → systolic augmentation.
③ Reduced Compliance
DPA is a muscular artery with less elastic tissue than the aorta. Same volume of blood generates higher pressure (P = V/C). Result: steeper upstroke, sharper peak, steeper downstroke. Less buffering of pulsatility.
④ Loss of Windkessel
The aorta stores energy in its elastic walls during systole and releases it during diastole — maintains diastolic pressure (Windkessel). Muscular arteries lack this elastic recoil → diastolic pressure falls more rapidly. Also explains why DBP drops at DPA.

Why does the dicrotic notch disappear?

Two reasons combined:
  • High-frequency damping: The sharp incisura is a high-frequency signal from aortic valve closure. Arterial walls progressively damp high-frequency components during transmission.
  • Wave reflection: Reflected waves from the periphery summate during diastole → replace the sharp notch with a broader diastolic wave.

Why is MAP preserved?

MAP = area under arterial waveform / time. Increased systolic and decreased diastolic largely cancel out. Loss is only ~1-2 mmHg from frictional resistance. MAP is the most reliable measurement across all arterial sites.

Calibration pearl: Static calibration determines MAP accuracy · Dynamic (frequency response) calibration determines SBP/DBP accuracy.

Effect of Ageing

Arterial stiffening with age → ↑ pulse wave velocity → reflected waves return earlier (during systole, not diastole) → augment systolic pressure centrally. Central and peripheral pressures converge in elderly. Loss of normal pulse pressure amplification gradient.

🃏 Active Recall

Q: Origin of dorsalis pedis artery?
A: Direct continuation of the anterior tibial artery, beginning at the inferior extensor retinaculum at the ankle joint line.
Q: Relations of DPA — medial vs lateral structures?
A: Medial: EHL tendon. Lateral: EDL tendons + deep peroneal nerve. Deep: talus → navicular → cuneiform.
Q: What % of population have absent DPA?
A: 3-12%. Replaced by branches from peroneal or posterior tibial arteries. Always do modified Allen test.
Q: Terminal branch of DPA?
A: Deep plantar artery — dives plantarward through 1st intermetatarsal space to complete the deep plantar arch (anastomosis with lateral plantar from PT).
Q: Name the 4 mechanisms of pulse pressure amplification.
A: ① Wave reflection ② Impedance mismatch ③ Reduced compliance ④ Loss of windkessel.
Q: Why is MAP similar at aorta and DPA?
A: MAP = area under waveform/time. ↑SBP and ↓DBP cancel out. Only ~1-2 mmHg drop from frictional resistance. MAP is the reliable trans-site measurement.
Q: What replaces the dicrotic notch at DPA?
A: A broader diastolic wave/hump. Two reasons: high-frequency damping of the sharp incisura, and summated wave reflections.
Q: What is the difference between incisura and dicrotic notch?
A: Incisura = sharp central (aortic) signal from aortic valve closure. Dicrotic notch = peripheral waveform feature replaced by a diastolic wave in distal sites.
Q: Pulse wave velocity in peripheral arteries?
A: 4-10 m/s. PWV ↑ with stiffer arteries → earlier reflected wave arrival.
Q: Approximate timing delay R-wave → DPA upstroke?
A: 180-200 ms (from aortic ejection). Reflects distance + PWV.
Q: How does ageing affect pulse pressure amplification?
A: Reduces it. Stiff arteries → ↑ PWV → reflected waves return earlier (during systole) → augment central SBP. Central and peripheral pressures converge.
Q: What calibration determines MAP vs SBP/DBP accuracy?
A: Static calibration → MAP accuracy. Dynamic (frequency response) calibration → SBP/DBP accuracy.
